# Historical precedent and performance evidence

Checked 2026-09-15. Paraphrased source notes; counts refer to the source’s own population and metric.

## Stevenson and Kording, 2011

- Source: [How advances in neural recording affect data analysis](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731), Nature Neuroscience 14, 139–142; published January 26, 2011.
- The 56-study historical sample tracked the upper range of simultaneously recorded single neurons. Figure 1 reports a doubling time of 7.4 ± 0.4 years. This is not a measurement of physical probes, commercial BCI electrode counts, or routine equipment ownership.
- The technology overview also includes imaging. Do not pool modern optical cell counts into a new invasive-electrical hardware trend.
- [Author’s tracking page](https://stevenson.lab.uconn.edu/scaling/) identifies its continuing scope as electrophysiology and provides source data/code links. Its live data were not refit for this brief.

## Urai and colleagues, 2022

- Source: [Large-scale neural recordings call for new insights to link brain and behavior](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00980-9), Nature Neuroscience 25, 11–19; published January 3, 2022.
- [Author-maintained code and data entry point](https://github.com/anne-urai/largescale_recordings) tracks simultaneously recorded neurons and links a dataset based on Stevenson’s collection. It provides a reproducible starting point for a subsequent systematic update, rather than evidence of a universal three-year clinical-BCI rule.
- No whole-brain extrapolation from this work is adopted as a forecast here.

## Willett and colleagues, 2023

- Source: [A high-performance speech neuroprosthesis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06377-x), Nature 620, 1031–1036; August 23, 2023.
- Four arrays were implanted; subsequent speech analyses used only the two arrays in ventral premotor area 6v. Thus implanted and decoded counts must be separated.
- Figure 4b’s offline channel-subsampling analysis reported an approximately 0.57 multiplier in word error rate per doubling of electrodes over the tested range.
- This is evidence that additional inputs can help a specific decoder in one participant. It does not establish a calendar-year hardware doubling rate or guarantee indefinite performance gains.

## Moore’s original analogy

- [Intel’s historical explanation](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/resources/moores-law.html) distinguishes the 1965 annual transistor-count prediction from the 1975 revision to roughly two years. Treat this as an empirical technology trend, not a physical law governing neurons or BCI performance.
